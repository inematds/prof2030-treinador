Lesson 7 · 20 min

Assessing the class: rubric, practical test, internal champions and follow-up

"I really liked it" on a satisfaction survey doesn't prove anyone learned. You need to see the person doing the work, against a clear standard, and come back weeks later to see whether the habit stuck. This lesson covers the 4-level rubric, the practical test, how to choose the internal champion, and the 30-, 60- and 90-day follow-up plan.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The 4 levels of evaluating a training
  2. 2A 4-level rubric for the delegate, review, approve cycle
  3. 3Who the internal champion is (and who they are not)
  4. 4The follow-up ritual that makes the habit stick

Goal: assess the class with a 4-level rubric and a practical test, choose the internal champion and set up the 90-day follow-up.

01 · What to measure, from easiest to what matters

The 4 levels of the Kirkpatrick model. Click each one.

Agree on the level 4 number before the workshop. Afterward, no one remembers what it was.

Almost all training measures only reaction. Yours measures at least learning and behavior.

02 · Test, champion and follow-up

Three pieces that turn a class into a habit.

1. Hands-on test

One task from the area: delegate a request, review an output with 3 planted errors and decide 3 actions (go ahead, approve, block). Same as the lesson 4 exercises, with a new case.

Score it with the rubric and give back the level with one sentence of feedback. A score without feedback doesn't teach.

FeedbackLevel 3 review: you found all 3 errors. To reach level 4, write the rule that would have prevented the wrong discount.

2. Internal champion

This is the person who watches usage every day, gathers questions and brings them to the weekly check-in. A good champion: has been at the company for over a year, has time set aside on their calendar, wants the role (their own words) and has the authority to make adjustments.

The wrong champion: someone who will leave in a few months, someone who already has too many duties, someone who was assigned without being asked.

At HorizonteJuliana uses the agent and the summaries every day: she's a natural candidate, if she wants the role and has time on her calendar. Or a senior attendant who scored level 3 or 4 on the test.

3. Weekly check-in

Same day and time every week. Fixed agenda: the week's numbers, questions from the log, one improvement and the next date.

A question that comes up 3 times becomes a 3-to-4-minute microvideo or a line in the 1-page guide.

Agenda5 min numbers · 10 min questions and adjustments · 5 min next week.

4. 30, 60, 90 days

Day 30: behavior (how many people use the cycle and how often). Day 60: a second short hands-on test, to see who moved up a level. Day 90: results against the number from before and a decision on the next step.

Recognize in public the people who brought results: people imitate what they see being valued.

Day 90 decisionKeep it, expand it to another area, or fix what didn't stick.

03 · Templates to copy

The rubric and the plan go in the lesson 8 kit.

4-level rubric · delegate, review, approve
RUBRIC · <class> · <date>
Levels: 1 Beginner · 2 Developing · 3 Proficient · 4 Reference

DELEGATE
1 vague request ("make a summary")
2 states the result, but with no context or format
3 result + context + format + what is off limits
4 level 3 + asks for proof (cite the source) and reuses it as a template

REVIEW
1 doesn't review, or only fixes grammar
2 finds 1 of the 3 planted errors
3 finds all 3 errors (fact, rule, personal data)
4 level 3 + writes the rule that prevents the error next time

APPROVE AND SECURITY
1 lets an irreversible action go out without approval
2 recognizes the high value, but not the injection or the personal data
3 classifies all 3 actions correctly (go ahead / approve / block)
4 level 3 + says who approves and what to record

APPLY AT WORK (day 30)
1 didn't use it
2 used it 1 or 2 times
3 uses it every week on the agreed task
4 uses it and taught a colleague or created a template for the team

Class goal: most people at level 3 in delegate and review on exam day.
90-day follow-up plan + champion
FOLLOW-UP · <company> · <area> · start <date>

INTERNAL CHAMPION
- Name: <...> · time at the company: <...> · time in their schedule: <30 min/day>
- Accepted the role in their own words? ( ) yes
- Backup if they leave: <name>

WEEKLY CHECK-IN · <fixed day and time> · 15-20 min
- 0-5 numbers · 5-15 questions from the log · 15-20 next week

USE AND QUESTIONS LOG (1 tab)
date | person | task | what the AI got wrong | what we did
- A question that comes up 3 times becomes a micro-video (3-4 min) or a line in the guide

INDICATORS (4 at most)
1. People using the cycle this week: <n of n>
2. Cases done with AI and review: <n>
3. Errors found in review: <n> (a rise at the start is a good sign)
4. Agreed result number: before <...> · now <...>

MILESTONES
- Day 30: behavior ("apply" rubric)
- Day 60: short practical exam
- Day 90: result vs. before · decision: keep / expand / fix

04 · Practice

Both pieces go into the lesson 8 kit.

1 · Your class rubric

20 min

Adapt the rubric to the area you chose in lesson 5: replace the examples at each level with situations from that area.

Done when: each criterion has 4 levels described by something you can observe, with no words like "good" or "satisfactory".

2 · Follow-up plan

20 min

Fill in the 90-day plan with a champion (real or from Horizonte), the day and time of the check-in, and the indicators.

Done when: the plan has a champion with a backup, a check-in with a day and time, 4 indicators at most, and the 3 milestones with dates.

Question 2 of 4

Question 3 of 4

06 · Summary

4 evaluation levelsReaction, learning, behavior, and result. Measure at least the middle ones.
4-level rubricDelegate, review, approve, and apply, with observable descriptions.
Internal championA real name, time in their schedule, willingness stated in their own words, a backup defined.
Follow-upA fixed weekly check-in and milestones at 30, 60, and 90 days.
