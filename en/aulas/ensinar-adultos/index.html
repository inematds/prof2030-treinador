Lesson 1 · 18 min

Teaching adults: the 5 principles that make the class use it on Monday

Adults don't learn AI by sitting through a lecture. They learn when they see the why, work on a problem of their own, practice with feedback, try to remember without looking, and use it again the following week. This lesson turns that into 5 principles and a lesson plan you can reuse in every workshop.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The 5 principles and where each one comes from
  2. 2Why an AI lecture doesn't change anyone's work
  3. 3How to swap a generic example for the learner's own task
  4. 4A ready-to-copy lesson plan

Goal: plan an AI lesson for adults with the 5 principles marked: why, real problem, practice, retrieval, and next week.

01 · The 5 principles, in lesson order

Each block is a moment in your lesson. Click to see what to do.

If you only have time for one principle, keep the real problem: it's what changes the outcome of an AI workshop the most.

Why and real problem hold attention; practice, retrieval, and next week make the habit stick.

02 · The 5 principles in the room

What changes in how you teach. Click each tab.

1. Why

Start with the cost of the problem, with a number from the company itself: "how many messages go unanswered after 6 p.m.?".

Name the fear early ("will this replace me?") and calmly say what the lesson changes and what it doesn't.

OpeningToday you're going to leave able to answer complaints in half the time, without losing control of what goes to the customer.

2. Real problem

Ask before the lesson: "bring a text, spreadsheet, or message you used this week" (no customer personal data).

Without learner material, use the lab company, with the industry vocabulary: order, route, invoice, exchange.

SwapInstead of "write a poem about AI", use "summarize yesterday's 5 complaints and say which one needs action today".

3. Practice and retrieval

Round 1: the person does it alone. You walk around and don't touch anyone's mouse. Round 2: they redo it with the feedback they received.

Before each new block, ask what stuck from the previous one. Wait 15 seconds for an answer.

QuestionWithout looking at the slide: what are the 3 things you check before sending the reply to the customer?

4. Next week

Each person writes down 1 task they will do with AI in the next 7 days and who they will show the result to.

Schedule a short follow-up (15 minutes, a few days later) with 2 retrieval questions and the cases from the week.

CommitmentBy Friday, I'll use the reply template on 10 complaints and show 2 of them to Juliana.

03 · Templates to copy

The lesson plan is your mold for every lesson in this program.

Lesson plan with the 5 principles
LESSON PLAN · <topic> · <class> · <duration>
Objective (verb + what the person can do afterward):
<...>

[1] WHY (up to 5 min)
- Problem the class already feels: <...>
- Company number that shows the cost: <...>
- Fear to name: <...>

[2] REAL PROBLEM
- Learner material requested beforehand: <...>
- Backup (lab company): <...>

[3] PRACTICE (round 1 and round 2)
- Task: <...>
- How the person knows they got it right: <...>
- Feedback I give between rounds: <...>

[4] RETRIEVAL
- 3 questions without looking at the material: <...> / <...> / <...>

[5] NEXT WEEK
- Each person's commitment: 1 task in 7 days
- Follow-up scheduled: <date> · 15 min

FINAL CHECK
[ ] the 5 principles appear above
[ ] more practice time than talking time
[ ] no real personal customer data in the exercise
Prompt · adapt the exercise to the class
I'm going to teach an AI lesson to <area and roles> at a <type and size of company>.
Tasks they do every week: <list 3 to 5>.

Create 3 hands-on 10-minute exercises, one per task, with:
1. the request the learner makes to the AI (with expected result and format);
2. what they should check in the response before using it;
3. how they know they got it right (done when).
Use the real vocabulary of the area. Do not use real personal data.
Don't invent company numbers: leave <number> where I should fill them in.

04 · Practice

Do it with a lesson you will actually teach.

1 · One lesson plan

30 min

Copy the lesson plan and fill it in for a 30- to 60-minute lesson about one use of AI at work for a class you know (or for the customer service team at Horizonte Distribuidora).

Done when: the plan is saved and each of the 5 principles has one line filled in and checked; practice takes up more time than explanation.

2 · Swap an example

10 min

Take a generic example you usually use ("ask for a poem", "ask for the capital of France") and swap it for a real task from the class's area, using the adaptation prompt.

Done when: you have 1 new exercise with a request, what to check, and a "done when" the person can verify on their own.

05 · Quick quiz

4 questions. The explanation appears after your attempt.

Question 1 of 4

The class is distracted in the first few minutes. Which principle was missing?

Question 2 of 4

You have 60 minutes. Which split follows the principles?

Question 3 of 4

At the end of the lesson, what best locks in the content?

Question 4 of 4

Why ask learners for a piece of their own work material before the lesson?

06 · Summary

Why and real problemOpen with the cost the class feels, and use their own task in the exercise.
Practice in two roundsDoing, getting feedback and redoing is worth more than seeing ten features.
RetrievalAsk before re-explaining; short quiz at the end.
Next weekCommitment to 1 task and a follow-up scheduled a few days later.
